## Evidence-Based Reasoning

### 1. Engagement Creates Retention
Response B uses dialogue-style engagement ("Imagine you and your best friend," "When YOU press your button") that makes a 10-year-old feel directly involved. Response A uses passive, textbook language. For a child, engagement isn't a bonus—it's essential for understanding that will stick. **Evidence**: "When YOU press your button" (direct address) vs. "Quantum entanglement is like" (abstract statement).

### 2. Honest Uncertainty Builds Credibility
Response B says: *"How does the box on the other side know what yours did SO FAST? Nobody really knows!"* This is scientifically honest AND exciting. It teaches kids that mysteries are the frontier of science. Response A implies a false certainty with the coin analogy, suggesting a complete understanding that doesn't actually exist. **Evidence**: Response A's coins "instantly know"—implying a mechanism. Response B's boxes display genuine mystery while remaining accurate.

### 3. Practical Meaning-Making

### 4. Tone Matches Audience
Response B uses capitalization, emphasis, and humor strategically ("spooky action at a distance—which is actually kind of a funny insult"). This isn't dumbing down—it's matching how children think: with excitement and wonder. Response A's neutral tone works for scientists, not 10-year-olds.

**To this:**

This version:
- Uses the more accurate box analogy
- Adds engagement and wonder
- Acknowledges honest uncertainty
- Stays accurate while being exciting
